    Step 2 · the transitions of the dynamics

    What moves together

    Each dynamic is a heatmap of the months it left its norm, and which way. Two dynamics are linked when a transformation carries one heatmap onto the other far more often than chance at the same moment of the record: the identity (the same way, the same months), a mirror (opposite ways), or a shift in time (one comes first). When it covers most of both, Eva takes them for one dynamic. Opposite trends and the borders found on the map belong here too.

    Every pair, as Eva weighed it

      How the detective works

      The classical tests

      • Rate of change: Sen's slope, the median of the slopes between every pair of years, computed on the yearly averages. A wild year cannot drag it.
      • Mann-Kendall test: does the series go up (or down) more often than chance would allow? It uses only the order of the values, with Hamed and Rao's correction for years that resemble their neighbours.
      • t-test on the least-squares slope, counting only the effective number of independent years.
      • Pettitt test: the year where the level jumped, if it did. Beware: a steady trend has no single level, and on a steady trend with realistic year-to-year persistence this test reports a « jump » 65 % of the time. The detective shows it, but asks Eva whether the dynamic really changed.
      • Years needed (Weatherhead et al. 1998): how long a trend of this size takes to stand out from the natural swings.

      Eva's molettes

      • Each variable is a dynamic. Eva places every month among the same months of her learning period (1981–2010): its position. Her molettes learn how positions follow each other (the norm) and which transitions are rare (the extremes).
      • She then reads every later month against that norm without learning from it, from the month before and from the month after.
      • At a coarser grain, a position only changes when a dynamic leaves its norm or comes back. The months it is out of its norm, and which way, form its heatmap. A link is a transformation that carries one heatmap onto another (same way, opposite way, or a few months later) far more often than chance read at the same moment of the record, so that a trend both share is not taken for a link. Every pair and every shift tried is counted against the evidence (Bonferroni).
      • A month out of its norm is often followed by others: joint months come in bunches. Each link is also read again with one series moved 1 to 5 years away, which keeps each one's persistence and most of its trend; a link must stand far above those readings.
      • The same transformation linking the same two variables in two regions is one process, even when their trends run opposite ways.
      • Did the dynamic change? Read month by month, a climate series fools any reader: a month resembles the one before. The level of a 45-year trend is the year. Eva reads the yearly values in levels of meta molettes (2, 4, 8… years joined): two neighbouring stretches are one dynamic unless chance would put them that far apart less than 1 % of the time, with the noise measured where it is, persistence included. On a steady trend with realistic noise she reports a change of regime 5.5 % of the time (68 series tested). Changes that several variables make together in one region are told, with how often chance would gather them.
      • Most series here come from one reanalysis, MERRA-2. A link between two of its variables can come from the model's own physics (the land model computes evaporation from soil water, for instance): each link says so when it can.
      • On the map, the trend of each grid cell is a dynamic too, and the step to its neighbour a transition. Out-of-norm transitions where the sign flips draw the borders where the trend reverses.

      What it cannot tell you

      • A link is not a cause. It says the same months move both dynamics; the process behind it is yours to find.
      • MERRA-2 is a reanalysis: a weather model kept on track by observations. Where observations are rare (polar seas, before satellites), it leans on the model.
      • A trend measured over 45 years says nothing certain about the next 45. Straight lines miss curves: check whether Eva sees more than one dynamic, and her departures.
